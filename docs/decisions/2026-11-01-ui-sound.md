# Interface sounds: three sets, mixed per sound, off until the person turns them on

Date: 2026-11-01. Decided by the owner.

## Decision

The owner listened to three playable sample sets (the sample page is in the project files,
`rust-ui/sound/sound-options.html`): **Ripple** (soft water drops, rising for "yes"/"open",
falling for "no"/"close"), **Glass** (bell-like chimes) and **Driftwood** (marimba and wood
block). They first picked Ripple, then asked for all three, with two rules:

- **Using sounds is the user's choice.** Sounds start off. Only the person using the app
  turns them on, with `SoundToggle` or `SoundSettings`; an app can't switch them on for them.
- **People mix and match.** In `SoundSettings` each person picks Ripple, Glass, Driftwood or
  Off for each sound. The app only sets the starting set (`SoundProvider { style }`) and
  which kinds exist: success, error and notice by default; taps, toggles and open/close only
  with `taps: true`.

Choices are stored in `localStorage` (`atoll-sound`, `atoll-sound-styles`). Minor sounds are
skipped under `prefers-reduced-motion` (browsers have no "reduced sound" setting). A sound
never carries meaning on its own; it always goes with something visible and announced.

## Why

UI sound guidance (Apple's HIG, Google's Material sound guide, Windows' sound schemes, WCAG's
sensory-characteristics and audio-control rules) agrees: few sounds, short, quiet, for
meaningful moments, always under the user's control. A click on every press tires people,
and extra sounds can talk over a screen reader.

## How

Web Audio from Rust (web-sys), no audio files and no JavaScript of ours. Desktop and native
are silent for now; a native backend would need an audio crate (licence check first).
